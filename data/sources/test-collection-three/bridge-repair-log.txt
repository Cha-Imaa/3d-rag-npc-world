The Millrace Bridge Repair Log

This log is invented test material for Loreland. The Millrace Bridge and everything described here are fictional.

The Millrace Bridge carries the main road across the river, just downstream of the mill. It has three stone arches. The middle arch is the widest, at 19 paces, and was rebuilt after the great flood. The two side arches are original and date from the year 405 of the village calendar.

The bridge is inspected every spring after the snowmelt, and again after any flood that reaches the second mark on the measuring post. The inspector walks under each arch at low water and taps every keystone with an iron rod. A clear ring means the stone is sound. A dull sound means the mortar behind it may be failing.

The worst damage on record came in the year 437, when floodwater carried a fallen oak into the middle arch. Two keystones cracked and the arch was closed to carts for the rest of that year. The repair took 11 weeks and used 46 new stones from the Greyfold quarry. A mason named Ansel Brook led the work.

Mortar for the bridge is mixed from lime, river sand, and crushed brick, in the ratio of one part lime to two parts sand to half a part brick. The crushed brick helps the mortar set under damp conditions. Mortar is never laid when frost is expected within three days, because frozen mortar crumbles.

Carts heavier than four oxen may not cross the bridge. Heavy loads must use the ford half a mile upstream, which is safe only in late summer. A painted post at each end of the bridge shows the weight rule in pictures, for travellers who cannot read the village script.

Moss is allowed to grow on the upstream face of the bridge, because the keepers believe it slows erosion. Ivy is cut back every autumn, because its roots force their way into the joints and open them up.
