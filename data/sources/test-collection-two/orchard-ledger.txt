The Brindlewood Orchard Ledger

This ledger is invented test material for Loreland. Brindlewood Orchard and everything described here are fictional.

Brindlewood Orchard lies on the south bank of the river, between the mill and the reed beds. It was planted by the Tallow family with 72 trees in the first year, and has grown to 214 trees today. The oldest tree still standing is a pear tree called the Grandmother, planted in the year 398 of the village calendar.

The orchard grows four kinds of fruit. There are apples, pears, quinces, and a small sour plum known locally as the hedge plum. Apples make up more than half of the orchard. The most common apple is the Russet Lantern, a brown-skinned apple that keeps through the winter when stored in straw.

Harvest begins on the first dry morning after the Feast of Ladders, which falls in early autumn. Pickers work from the north rows to the south rows, so the fruit that ripens first is picked first. A good harvest fills about 300 baskets. The best harvest in the ledger filled 418 baskets, in the year 449.

The orchard keeps bees in six wooden hives along the east wall. The hives are painted different colours so the keepers can tell them apart from a distance: blue, ochre, white, green, rust, and black. The black hive is the oldest and is said to make the darkest honey.

Frost is the greatest danger in spring. When a frost is expected, the keepers light small smudge fires between the rows before dawn. In the year 455 a late frost arrived in the Month of Blossom, and the orchard lost almost every pear blossom. The ledger records only 40 baskets of pears that year.

Fallen fruit is never wasted. Bruised apples go to the cider press behind the barn, and the pulp left over from pressing is fed to the pigs on the neighbouring farm. In return, the farm sends manure to the orchard each winter.
