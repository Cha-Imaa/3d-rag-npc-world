Grafting Notes from Brindlewood

These notes are invented test material for Loreland. They are fictional and are not real gardening advice.

Grafting at Brindlewood is done in late winter, while the trees are still asleep. The orchard keepers cut scion wood, which is a short length of young branch, from the healthiest trees in the Month of Ice. The scions are wrapped in damp moss and stored in the cool cellar under the barn until they are needed.

The orchard uses three grafting methods. The whip graft joins two stems of the same thickness with matching slanted cuts. The cleft graft splits a thicker stump and wedges two scions into the split. The bark graft slips a scion under the loosened bark of an older tree, and is used only in spring when the bark lifts easily.

Every graft is bound with linen tape and sealed with a wax made from beeswax and pine resin, mixed at a ratio of three parts wax to one part resin. The keepers say the resin keeps rain out of the cut, and the beeswax keeps the resin from cracking in the cold.

A graft that takes will show new buds within five weeks. The keepers mark each successful graft with a small copper tag that shows the year and the name of the parent tree. A graft that fails is cut back and tried again the following winter, but never more than twice on the same branch.

The most famous graft in the orchard is the Two-Fruit Tree near the gate. In the year 444 a keeper named Wren Tallow grafted pear scions onto a quince trunk, and the tree has carried both quinces and pears ever since. Children are allowed to pick one fruit from it at the end of each harvest.
