The Lantern Hill Weather Record

This record is invented test material for Loreland. Lantern Hill and everything described here are fictional.

The weather station on Lantern Hill was built in the year 412 of the village calendar by a surveyor named Odile Marsh. It stands 340 paces north of the old signal tower, beside a stone trough that collects rainwater for measurement. The first instrument was a copper rain gauge with a brass lid, and it is still in use.

Readings are taken twice a day, at the first bell and at the evening bell. Each reading notes the rainfall, the wind direction, and the colour of the sky at the horizon. The keepers write in grey ink for ordinary days and red ink for storm days, so a storm can be found quickly when paging through the books.

The wettest month on record was the Month of Reeds in the year 437, when the gauge collected 61 thumbs of rain. The bridge road flooded for nine days that month, and the market moved to the hilltop meadow until the water went down. The driest year on record was 451, with only 14 thumbs of rain across the whole summer.

Wind on Lantern Hill usually comes from the west. An east wind is rare, and the keepers call it the Salt Wind because it smells of the coast, which is three days away on foot. The Salt Wind blew for an entire week only once, in the autumn of 446, and the record notes that every weathervane in the village turned at the same moment.

Fog is common in early spring. The keepers measure fog by how many fence posts can be counted from the station door. A fog of fewer than four posts is written as a heavy fog. Heavy fog closes the hill path to carts, though walkers may still pass with a lantern.

The station has had five keepers in total. The current keeper took over in the year 458 and has kept every book in a cedar chest under the station bench. Visitors may read the books, but the keepers ask that nobody copies the red-ink pages without permission.
